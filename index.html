Illiquid Assets?
Illiquid (Net) = Total NW − Liquid Assets

This covers Property equity, CPF, Insurance and any other non-liquid assets — minus all liabilities (home loan, car loan, credit cards).

Liquid + Illiquid always equals your Total Net Worth.

Expected Portfolio Return %/yr?
Blended portfolio return — the weighted average return across all your investable assets.

This rate applies to your total net worth (excluding property if you select No below).

Typical blended assumptions for Singapore:
• CPF earns a fixed 2.5–4% — this pulls the average down
• Equities/ETFs: 6–8%
• Cash/FDs/SSBs: 2.5–4%

A realistic blended rate for a typical SG portfolio is 3–5%.

Set conservatively — better to be pleasantly surprised.
Include property in portfolio??
No = more conservative and realistic for most Singaporeans.

If your property is your home and you don't plan to sell or downgrade, its growth is paper wealth — you can't spend it in retirement.

Select Yes only if you plan to downgrade, sell, or rent out the property to fund retirement.
Before Retirement
Take-Home Income/mo?
Use your total monthly income including CPF contributions.

CPF contributions count — they go toward your retirement, just in a separate bucket.

Formula:
Monthly income = Annual gross ÷ 12

Example:
Base salary S$11,910/mo × 15 months (inc. 3 months bonus) = S$178,650/yr
S$178,650 ÷ 12 = S$14,888/mo

This gives a more accurate picture of how much is building toward your retirement each month.
Monthly Surplus Invested?
Cash surplus you invest each month.

Auto-calculated as Income − Expenses when both are filled.

To override manually — for example to deduct mortgage principal repayment which goes toward loan reduction, not investment:

Income − Expenses − Mortgage principal = investable surplus

Your manual value is saved and will persist. Tap the field and clear it to reset to auto.
↺ Clear to reset to auto
Monthly Expenses?
Your total monthly expenses at retirement age.

If your mortgage is still ongoing at retirement:
• Include the interest portion only
• Exclude the principal repayment — it reduces your loan balance, not a true expense

Example: Monthly instalment S$3,500 (S$1,200 interest + S$2,300 principal)
→ Include only S$1,200 as expense
Return on Surplus %/yr?
Conservative return on your monthly surplus.

Your existing portfolio compounds at the Portfolio Return rate above.

Your new monthly savings may earn less — they often sit in cash, CPF OA (2.5%) or SSBs (3%) before being deployed.

Recommended: 2–3%. Default: 2.5%.

Monthly Surplus?
Money you still set aside after retiring.

Examples:
• Part-time work surplus
• Rental income reinvested
• Dividends not spent

This reduces your monthly drawdown from your portfolio, so you need a smaller nest egg.

Leave at 0 if you expect no surplus after expenses.
Other Income/mo?
Income you receive in retirement beyond CPF Life.

Examples:
• Rental income
• Part-time / freelance
• Dividends paid out
• Spouse's income

This offsets your expenses so you draw less from your portfolio.
CPF Life at 65/mo?
CPF LIFE pays monthly income from age 65 for life.

Log in to my.cpf.gov.sg → My Retirement → CPF LIFE Estimator

Typical: S$700–S$2,000/mo
Return on Surplus %/yr?
Return on your reinvested savings after retirement.

Recommended: 2–3% conservative.
Set equal to Portfolio Return if fully reinvested in equities.
Reduce exp. after 65??
No = most conservative estimate (recommended).

Keeping expenses flat assumes your costs don't drop after 65 — this gives you the largest required nest egg and the safest plan.

Yes applies a 15% reduction after 65, reflecting that home loans are typically paid off by then. However healthcare costs rise, so the actual saving is modest.

If in doubt, select No.
